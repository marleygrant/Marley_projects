04 — Selected Work

Analysis that ends in a recommendation

Every project below follows the same shape: a messy real-world dataset, a model built to answer a specific business question, and a set of recommendations someone could actually use.

Regional Hospital Patient-Volume Forecast

Team project — Excel, PivotTables, Regression

Context

A regional hospital needed a three-month (Nov–Jan) staffing and resource forecast, built from 28 months of daily patient census data.

My Role

Assisted in seasonal-index calculation and regression forecasting, then led interpretation of the results into recommendations for hospital leadership.

Approach

Isolated day-of-week and monthly seasonality with PivotTable-derived seasonal indices, deseasonalized the series, fit a linear trendline, forecasted forward, then reseasonalized by month and day.

Outcome

Identified January as the peak month (post-holiday visits and flu season) and Wednesday/Thursday as peak days — recommended midweek staffing increases, expanded January appointment capacity, and advance supply planning.

Econometric Analysis of Global Life Expectancy

Independent project — R, FGLS, Panel Data

Context

Panel data across 150+ countries, used to identify what actually drives life expectancy at a national level.

My Role

Built and compared 10+ regression specifications in R, iterating on model design end to end.

Approach

Added interaction terms and nonlinear effects, then applied FGLS correction to address heteroskedasticity across the panel.

Outcome

Final model reached up to 77% adjusted R², translated into policy recommendations tied to education, mortality, GDP per capita, and resource access.

Full presentation deck and supporting workbooks available on request.

05 — Perspective

What I've noticed working both sides of the data

01

Most operational data isn't clean — it's a produce case at 6am or a hospital admit log. Consulting engagements live or die on whether the analyst can work with data as it actually shows up, not the tidy version in a textbook.

02

A forecast only creates value once someone changes a staffing schedule or a supply order because of it. I've sat on both ends of that handoff — as the person building the model and the person who'd have to act on it.

03

Firms increasingly need generalists who can move between statistical rigor and plain-language recommendations in the same meeting. That's the muscle I've been building since day one on the retail floor.
